‘Who wants to be hired’ now outnumbers ‘Who is hiring’

For the first time since the monthly threads began pairing in 2014, job-seeker comments on Hacker News exceed hiring comments.
Ilia Kolesnikov · July 2026 · data and code

Every month Hacker News runs two job threads. Companies post openings in “Ask HN: Who is hiring?” and people looking for work post in “Who wants to be hired?”. The comment counts make a small but honest labor-market indicator: nobody advertises a job that doesn’t exist, and nobody asks for work they don’t need.

I pulled the counts for every thread from the HN Algolia API and checked the pairs back to 2014. In 143 months, seeker comments never once outnumbered hiring comments. In 2026 they did.

The timeline is the part most retellings get wrong. Hiring comments fell 35% during 2022, months before ChatGPT launched. The break came in July 2022, right after the Fed’s first 75-basis-point hike since 1994 and the big-tech hiring freezes. And the Fed has been cutting since September 2024 with no rebound at all. The decline didn’t start with AI. The missing recovery is where AI becomes the suspect.

Averaged over January–July, 2021 had 961 hiring comments a month against 219 from seekers. In 2026 it’s 488 against 502. The flip came in May and widened every month since: ahead by 5 in May, 91 in June, 129 in July.

The threads aren’t dying, which would be the boring explanation. Both threads together have held near 11–13k comments a year since 2019. What changed is who shows up. Job seekers wrote 19% of the comments in 2021. This year they’ve written 51%.

Comment counts are a proxy. One hiring comment can hide fifty openings, and HN skews senior and startup-heavy. The July 2026 thread is still accumulating, so today’s numbers will drift up. Data, fetch script, and chart code: github.com/eli-kolesnikov/hn-hiring-crossover.
