# ‘Who wants to be hired’ now outnumbers ‘Who is hiring’

For the first time since 2014, [job-seeker comments](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48747975) on [Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com) exceed [hiring comments](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48747976).

Ilia Kolesnikov · July 2026 · [data and code](https://github.com/ej29-r3d/hn-hiring-crossover)

Every month [Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com) runs two job threads: job postings go in “[Ask HN: Who is hiring?](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48747976)” and resumes in “[Who wants to be hired?](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48747975)”.

I counted the comments in every thread and checked the pairs back to 2014. In 143 months, seeker comments never once outnumbered hiring comments. In 2026 they did.

{{chart hiring-chart.html?embed=1&title=hide&deck=hide 640}}

The drop tracks Fed rates, not AI. Hiring fell 35% during 2022, before ChatGPT launched, and none of the AI releases marked on the chart left a visible break.

{{chart avg-by-year.html?embed=1 560}}

Averaged over January–July, 2021 had 961 hiring comments a month against 219 from seekers. In 2026 it’s 488 against 502. The flip came in May and widened every month since: ahead by 5 in May, 91 in June, 129 in July.

{{chart seeker-share.html?embed=1 540}}

The threads aren’t dying, which would be the boring explanation. Both threads together have held near 11–13k comments a year since 2019. What changed is who shows up. Job seekers wrote 19% of the comments in 2021. This year they’ve written 51%.

---

Comment counts are a proxy: one hiring comment can hide fifty openings, and HN skews senior and startup-heavy. Data and chart code: [github.com/ej29-r3d/hn-hiring-crossover](https://github.com/ej29-r3d/hn-hiring-crossover).
